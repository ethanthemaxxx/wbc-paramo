We forecast the water that comes down from the páramo.

Give every town downstream a calendar for its water.

Páramo is a small lab in Bogotá. We work with the people who run water systems, irrigate land and plan cities, and we build the forecasts they ask us for.

Built with partners across the Andean highlands (fictional, for this concept)

Where we started

We began with a question from a water operator: how long will the reservoir last?

Our founders were working on two sides of the same mountain. One was a hydrologist who spent her weekends walking páramo streams. The other built forecasting models for a living.

Both kept hearing the same question from people downstream. Not “will it rain?” but “how much water will we have, and for how long?” Rain maps could not answer it. The mountain holds water for weeks before it lets it go.

So we started with the mountain. We put sensors beside the frailejones, added satellite and rainfall records, and taught a model how each watershed remembers. It answers in ranges, and it tells you when it is unsure.

We are still small on purpose. We would rather know a few watersheds well than claim to know them all.

Mariana Quiroga Salcedo
Tomás Arbeláez

Our values

  • Listen first

    We walk the watershed and talk to the people who use it before we write a line of code.

  • Say how sure we are

    Every forecast shows its range. A wide range is information, and we do not hide it.

  • Stay local

    We build with the utilities, farmers and communities who live downstream, and we keep their data theirs.
